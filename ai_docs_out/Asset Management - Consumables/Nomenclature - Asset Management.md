## Type (Vendor)

<!-- id: type-vendor | category: Asset Management -->

**What it does**

The Vendor Type is a mandatory field where at least one of the following checkboxes must be selected: Organization, Warranty Provider, Insurance Provider, Re Sale Buyer, Service Provider, Seller. This ensures that the vendor is categorized correctly based on the services offered by them.

**Why it matters**

Categorizing vendors by type is essential for understanding their role in the asset lifecycle, facilitating targeted communication, and ensuring appropriate management based on the services they provide.

**How to use**

1. 1. When adding a vendor, select at least one checkbox from the available types.
2. 2. Choose 'Organization' if the vendor is a general company.
3. 3. Select 'Warranty Provider' if they offer warranties.
4. 4. Choose 'Insurance Provider' for insurance services.
5. 5. Select 'Re Sale Buyer' if they purchase assets for resale.
6. 6. Choose 'Service Provider' for maintenance or other services.
7. 7. Select 'Seller' if they sell products or assets.

**Examples**

- Selecting 'Seller' and 'Service Provider' for a vendor that both sells and maintains equipment.

## Consumable Asset

<!-- id: consumable-asset | category: Asset Management -->

**What it does**

Consumable Assets in asset management refer to items that are used up or depleted during their use and need to be replaced periodically. These assets have a short lifespan and are typically low-cost items that don't hold long-term value or depreciation. ## Batches (Consumable Assets)

<!-- id: batches | category: Asset Management -->

**What it does**

Batches are groups of consumable items produced, purchased, or received together, typically identified by a Batch ID. This helps institutions track items based on production or procurement timelines, ensuring better inventory management, expiry tracking, and quality control. It includes 'Batch ID' (a unique identifier) and 'Batch Quantity' (the total number of units within a specific batch).

**Why it matters**

Batch tracking is crucial for managing consumable assets, especially those with expiry dates or quality variations, enabling precise inventory control, efficient recall management, and adherence to 'First Expired First Out' (FEFO) principles.

**Examples**

- A batch of 100 pens received on 2023-01-15 with Batch ID 'PEN-2023-001'.
